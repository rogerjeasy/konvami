# Competitor gap analysis (2026-11-20)

What other language apps do that Konvami does not, ranked by learning benefit, by how much it keeps learners coming back, and by effort. It also covers which motivation mechanics to adopt, what an "AI coach" could do with Konvami's data, and which English, French and Italian speaking exams to simulate first.

**Method.** I took Konvami's current state from `docs/design/README.md` (the copy on disk, which is newer than older summaries: it includes 5.24 Two partners, 5.25 Phrase bank, ÖSD, TestDaF, offline review and per-sound pronunciation scores), from spec §6–§7, and from searching `apps/mobile/src` and `services/api`. Before calling anything "Missing" I searched the code for it. Competitor facts come from web searches made on 2026-11-20. Many review sites are affiliate pages, so I preferred official pages and app-store listings. Anything I could not confirm against a source is marked **(unverified)**. Where I describe well-known product mechanics without a fresh source, I say so.

## 1. Competitors at a glance

**Duolingo** (gamified course for the mass market)

- Video Call with Lily (Max tier only). Lily adapts to your level and does not correct grammar. Calls last about 1 minute early in a course and up to 3 minutes later. Languages are EN, ES, FR, DE, IT and PT, with JA and KO on iOS. [blog.duolingo.com/video-call](https://blog.duolingo.com/video-call). A 2025 update added "Lily Calls You" (Lily calls the learner from time to time to nudge practice) and call transcripts, per [StoryLearning](https://storylearning.com/duolingo-max-review) **(not confirmed on a Duolingo page)**.
- Streaks, streak freezes, Friend Streaks, weekly leagues, quests and lock-screen and home-screen widgets. Duolingo's own data: learners with at least one Friend Streak are **22% more likely** to finish their daily lesson ([product-lessons-friend-streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/), Sept 2024). The widgets come in four families, two for the lock screen and two for the home screen ([blog.duolingo.com/widget-feature](https://blog.duolingo.com/widget-feature)).
- In 2025 Duolingo replaced hearts with "energy", which every exercise uses up. Duolingo reported more daily users and more time spent, and free users pushed back hard, calling it a "cash grab" ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/quitting-duolingo-energy-system-3599842/), [Q2 2025 call](https://www.aol.com/finance/duolingo-duol-q2-2025-earnings-230249111.html)).
- 2026: Explain My Answer and Speaking Adventures became free; DuoRadio and Advanced Stories for listening and reading; Image Flashcards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/22/duolingo-is-now-giving-users-access-to-advanced-learning-content/), [Oct 2026 update](https://blog.duolingo.com/october-2026-update/)). Duolingo is reported to have tested a tool that locks other apps until the daily lesson is done ([pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/edtech/news/duolingo-experiments-with-tool-to-lock-other-apps-until-daily-lessons-are-complete-e53a242c-79a5-4419-a8e1-aa44e1212595)) **(unverified)**.
- Notifications are chosen per learner by a bandit algorithm that prefers "fresh" templates. It gave **+0.5% daily active users and +2% new-user retention** (Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020, [PDF](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)).

**Babbel** (structured course)

- Babbel Speak (Sept 2025, still labelled beta) runs scripted AI scenarios with prompts and feedback ([press](https://www.babbel.com/press/en-us/releases/babbel-speak)). Babbel Live group classes stopped for consumers in mid-2025 and remain only in Babbel for Business ([heise](https://heise.de/-10647158); exact date **unverified**). A review manager uses spaced repetition, and lessons work offline.

**Busuu** (course with community features)

- Community Corrections: native speakers correct learners' spoken and written answers ([blog](https://blog.busuu.com/community-corrections/)). AI Conversations correct the learner at the end, not during the conversation ([help](https://help.busuu.com/hc/en-us/articles/21862192336402)). Study plan, offline use, and a CEFR certificate with McGraw Hill ([Univext comparison](https://univext.com/en/blog/365/babbel-vs-busuu-2026), affiliate).

**Memrise**

- The 2025 redesign added a "Videos" tab of native-speaker clips matched to the words being learned, plus MemBot conversations ([blog](https://www.memrise.com/blog/changes-to-the-memrise-app)). It also has helper bots for grammar, role-play, conjugation and culture ([copycatcafe](https://copycatcafe.com/memrise/), unverified).

**Speak** (voice-first AI course backed by OpenAI's fund)

- Lessons, speaking drills, role-play, free talk, Speak Tutor Q&A, Smart Review and a phrasebook. "Made for You" lessons built from the learner's mistakes are Premium Plus only ([LanguaTalk review, Nov 2026](https://languatalk.com/blog/speak-app-review/)). Reviewers say the feedback is brief, speech recognition is too lenient, and there is no structured grammar.
- **AI Tutor Calls** (Oct 2026, Premium Plus, English and Spanish only): one-to-one tutor calls modelled on its business product. Premium costs about $18 a month and Premium Plus about $40 a month ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/speak-ai-tutor-call-3725246)).

**Praktika**

- Animated 3D avatar tutors. Version 4.0 (Feb 2026) brought "multi-agent AI" that analyses goals, tracks progress and adjusts the study plan, plus new tutors, dark mode and better VoiceOver support ([praktika.ai/blog/praktika-4-0](https://praktika.ai/blog/praktika-4-0)). Reviewers say it is light on grammar.

**Loora** (English only): conversations at the learner's level, a 5-minute spoken placement, and business modules for interviews and meetings ([loora.com](https://www.loora.com/lp/ai-english-tutor); details from directories, partly **unverified**).

**ELSA Speak** (English only): phoneme-level pronunciation scoring by accent, prep courses for IELTS, TOEFL, TOEIC, PTE and EIKEN, and a "Speech Analyzer" that estimates IELTS and TOEFL bands ([elsaspeak.com](https://elsaspeak.com/?p=16114); its availability is **unverified**).

**Talkpal**: many modes (chat, call, role-play, debates, historical "characters", photo). An aggregator claims exam simulations for IELTS, TOEFL and DELF ([similarlabs](https://similarlabs.com/fr/p/talkpal-ai), **unverified**). Reviewers call the debates shallow ([LanguaTalk](https://languatalk.com/blog/talkpal-review/)).

**Langua (LanguaTalk)**: the closest competitor to Konvami. It has optional memory of the learner's interests, voices cloned from native speakers, feedback reports, flashcards from conversations, **AI stories built from the learner's saved words**, import of texts and audio, podcasts and videos at the learner's level, debates, and a hands-free call mode. The Standard plan caps calls at 30 minutes a day, and practice reminders are the only consistency feature ([languatalk.com/ai-language-tutor](https://languatalk.com/ai-language-tutor)).

**Gliglish**: browser and mobile apps, 50+ scenarios, speaking speed control, 10 free minutes a day, Plus from about $29 a month ([press kit](https://gliglish.com/press-kit)).

**Lingvist**: adaptive vocabulary drills with a placement test and custom decks built from a text. No speaking ([FluentU](https://www.fluentu.com/blog/lingvist-review/)).

**Pimsleur**: audio-first lessons of about 30 minutes, an AI Voice Coach that scores pronunciation 1–100, and an AI Conversation Coach in beta for some languages ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1405735469), [blog](https://www.pimsleur.com/blog/why-ai-alone-cant-teach-you-a-language-and-how-pimsleur-bridges-the-gap/)).

**Rosetta Stone / Mondly**: Rosetta Stone has TruAccent speech recognition, Stories, a Phrasebook and live coaching ([rosettastone.com](https://www.rosettastone.com/features/truaccent-speech-recognition)). Mondly has a chatbot and VR/AR lessons; reviewers call it basic ([testprepinsight](https://testprepinsight.com/comparisons/mondly-vs-rosetta-stone/)).

**Preply / italki** (human tutors, the benchmark): Preply's AI **Lesson Insights** writes a structured report of each recorded lesson (speaking time, words, grammar), and Daily Exercises and Scenario Practice fill the time between lessons ([Preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/preply-announces-new-ai-powered-features-to-guide-the-future-of-personalized-learning-in-a-human-ai-world), [OpenAI case study](https://openai.com/index/preply)). I found no comparable italki AI feature.

**LingQ / Clozemaster / Anki**: LingQ is built on importing content you read and listen to; the free tier allows 5 imports and 20 saved words ([Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/best-lingq-alternatives/)). Clozemaster drills words in context (cloze sentences) with a radio-style listening mode. Anki with FSRS defaults to 90% desired retention, and FSRS predicts recall far better than the old SM-2 scheduler in the open srs-benchmark ([domenic.me](https://domenic.me/fsrs/); figures **unverified**).

**Newcomers in 2025–2026**

- **Google Translate Practice** (Aug 2025 beta, free): scenario listening and speaking adapted to level, for EN↔ES/FR/PT ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/translate/language-learning-live-translate/)). This is a free competitor for beginners.
- **Cambridge Speak & Improve** (free research tool): five practice tests modelled on Linguaskill Speaking, with an estimated CEFR speaking level ([help](https://help.writeandimprove.com/en/articles/4401879-about-speak-improve-a-research-project)).
- **Goethe-Institut KOSI** chatbot: about 76,000 conversations in Goethe online courses between Oct 2025 and Jul 2026 ([goethe.de](https://www.goethe.de/prj/dlp/de/magazin-sprache/27358624.html)).
- A wave of single-exam apps for TCF and TEF Canada (TCF Exam Hero, Akseli, OralPrep, Talkparty and others). Each simulates the Tâche 1–3 or Section A/B speaking tasks with AI scoring; none is official ([search results](https://apps.apple.com/gr/app/id6772313115)).

**Reading the matrix.** Konvami already covers more of the learning loop than any single competitor (verified corrections, mistake memory, SRS that feeds back into conversation, exams, import, memory, pen-pal). Its gaps are mainly in **reasons to come back** (streak protection, widgets, social features, incoming calls), **passive input** (stories and listening), **exams outside German**, and **accent and dialect variety**, which matters in Switzerland.

## 4. Retention and motivation

### What the evidence says

| Mechanic                          | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Verdict for Konvami                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Streaks**                       | Seven studies: an intact streak that is shown to the learner raises later engagement compared with a broken one. The harm is larger when people blame themselves for the break and smaller when the streak can be **repaired** (Silverman & Barasch, _J. Consumer Research_ 2023, [summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). The effect is modest: about 66% of people continued with an intact streak against 58% after a break ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)). Streak incentives also beat larger, flat incentives for persistence (Mehr et al., _OBHDP_ 2025, per the same search, **unverified**). | Keep the streak, and add **planned rest days and a speaking repair** (gap 4). Never show a broken streak as a loss.                                        |
| **Streak freeze**                 | A claimed −21% churn among at-risk users comes from a portfolio case study with no primary source ([jiwonvyoo](https://jiwonvyoo.github.io/portfolio/case/duolingo/duo.html)), so **unverified**. Separating the streak from the daily goal ("one lesson counts") is reported to have raised 7-day streaks by 40%+ (teardown, **unverified**).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Use the low-bar principle: any speaking counts, even one pen-pal reply.                                                                                    |
| **Social accountability**         | Friend Streak: +22% daily lesson completion (Duolingo's own data, [blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/)). In the gamification meta-analysis, social interaction moderates behavioural outcomes (Sailer & Homner 2020, [paper](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Worth doing later in a minimal, non-competitive form (gap 13). Owner-dependent.                                                                            |
| **Gamification overall**          | g = 0.49 cognitive, 0.36 motivational and 0.25 behavioural (Sailer & Homner 2020, via [secondary summaries](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/edtech-growth/gamification-in-learning)). Leaderboards alone: SMD about 0.27 ([2022 meta-analysis](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202230853598218.pdf)). In programming education only **levels** had a significant effect ([ISCTE](https://repositorio.iscte-iul.pt/bitstream/10071/29213/1/article_95533.pdf)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Levels and progress towards them (gap 8) are better supported than leaderboards.                                                                           |
| **Notifications**                 | Optimising content with a novelty-aware bandit gave +0.5% daily users and +2% new-user retention. Repeating the same template wears off ([Yancey & Settles 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Vary the wording through the partner's own voice and memories (Konvami already writes event follow-ups). The gains come from relevance, not from pressure. |
| **Goal-setting and fresh starts** | Implementation intentions ("when X, I will Y") and the fresh-start effect (Mondays, new months) are well established in the behavioural literature (general knowledge, not re-fetched here).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Konvami already asks for a weekly goal and a reminder time. Add a fresh-start message on Monday ("new week, new plan") instead of "you missed…".           |

### What backfires or feels manipulative

- **Guilt and shame notifications** (the "sad owl" style). Spec §16.3 already forbids guilt-tripping in reminders.
- **Mistakes that cost something** (hearts). They punish exactly the risk-taking that speaking needs, which goes against spec §14.3 and the "Let's polish this" rule.
- **Energy and paywalled continuation.** Users saw them as a cash grab ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/quitting-duolingo-energy-system-3599842/)).
- **Leagues.** They reward volume (easy lessons repeated) over speaking, and losing a league is an "external" failure that wears down motivation for learners who are not competitive.
- **Paid streak repair, app-locking and fake urgency** ("your partner is sad"). With an AI persona this is especially manipulative, and it conflicts with the §16.3 rule against manipulative dynamics.

### Recommendation for Konvami

1. **A streak you cannot lose to life.** The streak counts days the plan asked for, and rest days are fine. A miss can be repaired once a week by speaking: a voice note or a 2-minute call. Wording is always forward-looking ("Pick it up with Lena?"), never red, never a loss counter.
2. **Progress you can see.** "3 of 5 conversations point to B1" (gap 8), mistakes that faded on the grammar map, and Then vs Now. These are intrinsic signals of competence, which is the strongest long-term motivator.
3. **Relationship, not pressure.** Partners that remember, ask about the dentist appointment and (opt-in) ring you (gap 5) give a reason to come back that no badge can. Cap it at one proactive contact a day across reminders, pen-pal notes and calls.
4. **Small weekly quests** made of real speaking, with no currency (gap 12). Milestones get dates (the README says they are undated today).
5. **Respectful win-back.** Keep the single quiet-week nudge, and make it a partner's voice note about something the learner cares about, with a one-tap "pause reminders for a month".
6. **Measure it.** Use PostHog (opt-in, EU) to count D1, D7 and D30 retention and weekly speaking minutes per variant before adding anything heavier.

## 5. An "AI coach" agent

### What competitors do

- **Praktika 4.0:** "multi-agent AI" that analyses goals, tracks progress and adjusts the plan ([blog](https://praktika.ai/blog/praktika-4-0)). It is a black box, with no visible reasoning.
- **Speak "Made for You":** personalised lessons from frequent mistakes, Premium Plus only ([review](https://languatalk.com/blog/speak-app-review/)).
- **Busuu:** a study plan and an AI revision assistant that targets weak areas.
- **Duolingo:** "Birdbrain" adaptive difficulty and bandit-chosen reminders (general knowledge and KDD paper). It optimises engagement, not visible coaching.
- **Preply Lesson Insights:** a structured report after each lesson, plus daily exercises picked from it ([Preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/part-ii-how-preply-uses-ai-to-give-you-personalized-lesson-insights)).

### What Konvami already has (more than most)

Reports with confirmed mistakes (`reports.py`), the grammar map's per-topic uses and errors (`grammar_map.py`), the level engine and its evidence (`levels.py`), FSRS cards and review history (`fsrs.py`), the week's plan and goals (`director.py`), pace in words per minute (`fluency.py`), support signals such as "slower please" (`learning_signals.py`), the weekly check-in (`week_review.py`), exam readiness (`exam_centre.py`), memories and events (`memories.py`), and pronunciation scores per sound (`pronunciation_assessment.py`).

### What to add: a visible coach, not a new chatbot

Keep the Director deterministic (free, instant, testable) and add a **"Why this, today"** layer plus a few rules that act on signals:

1. **Explain each recommendation in one line, with its evidence.** For example: "Dativ after _mit_ came up 4 times in your last 3 calls; today Lena will steer towards it." The data already exists in the planner, so the coach makes it visible.
2. **Stuck-topic detection.** A topic whose mistakes have not fallen over 3 weeks triggers a guided lesson (5.15) instead of more free talk. A topic that is improving moves into review conversations.
3. **Pace and support signals.** Rising "slower please" or falling words per minute means suggesting an easier partner or shorter calls. Never infer emotion from the voice (spec §6.9).
4. **Card-load balancing.** If the due count is more than about 3× the daily review rate, pause new-card suggestions on reports and schedule a review conversation.
5. **Exam countdown coaching.** Readiness by part, then the weakest part, then a drill of that part only (a 3-minute call), with phrase-bank cards (5.25).
6. **Goal realism check.** If the level goal by its date needs more minutes than the plan allows, say so once and offer to move the date or add a day.
7. **Monthly letter.** Once a month a partner records a 60-second "how you've changed" message from Then vs Now and the level path. It uses the small text model and read-aloud, as the weekly check-in does today.

Each of these rules is a pure function over existing tables, easy to unit test and cheap. Only the wording uses a model. The learner can always ask "why?" and see the evidence, which keeps the coach honest (spec §16.1).

### What to simulate first (Switzerland, Germany, France)

1. **fide (DE, FR, IT).** Unique to the first market, legally required for residence permits and naturalisation in Switzerland, served by no AI competitor I found, and one format covers three of Konvami's languages. This is the clearest gap Konvami could own.
2. **TCF (tout public and IRN) and DELF B1/B2.** France raised the language bar on 1 Jan 2026: A2 for a first multi-year permit, B1 for the 10-year card, B2 for naturalisation ([Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/france-immigration-2026-language-requirements), cross-check on service-public.fr). TCF fits one call exactly. DELF B1 is also accepted for Swiss naturalisation in French-speaking cantons.
3. **IELTS Speaking and Cambridge B2 First / C1 Advanced.** These are the most demanded English exams among Swiss and German students and professionals. IELTS needs no second voice, and Cambridge reuses 5.17's second candidate.
4. Later: TEF (mostly a Canada market), CELI and CILS (Ticino and Italy), TOEFL 2026 (short and well served by ETS and others), DALF C1. Skip PTE, which is item drills already served by many apps.

### Trademark and claim cautions

- **Use exam names only to describe, never as Konvami's product name or in the logo.** Write "practice for the speaking part of the IELTS test", not "IELTS Speaking Simulator". ETS's published guidelines are a useful template: the mark is never shown alone, every page carries a notice ("TOEFL is a registered trademark of ETS. This product is not endorsed or approved by ETS"), and titles take the form "XYZ preparation for the TOEFL® test" ([ETS](https://www.etscanada.ca/trademark_guidelines.html)). IELTS marks belong to the IELTS Partners, whose materials are licensed for personal, non-commercial use only ([ielts.org](https://ielts.org/legal/ielts-copyright-and-trade-mark-statement)). Cambridge marks belong to Cambridge University Press & Assessment. DELF, DALF and TCF are run by France Éducation international and TEF by CCI Paris Île-de-France. I could not confirm their trademark registrations: **the owner should check EUIPO, WIPO and Swissreg, and have a lawyer review the store listing**.
- **Never copy official tasks, pictures or rubrics.** Konvami's German rule ("the tasks are our own, after the public descriptions", 5.16–5.18 row) should apply to every exam.
- **Scores are estimates.** Never say "IELTS band 7" or "you will pass". Say "estimated, based on public band descriptors", as 5.18 does now. ELSA's and Cambridge's own tools give the same warning.
- **fide** is a programme of the Swiss State Secretariat for Migration (SEM). Avoid any suggestion of official status or of a "fide-approved" result.
- **Store review:** App Store guideline 5.2 (intellectual property) can reject listings that use third-party marks prominently. Keep the marks out of the app name and subtitle.
